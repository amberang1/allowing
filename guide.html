A practical guide to releasing

One feeling at a time.

Choose your practice

Releasing feelings guides the three questions. Releasing wants guides the first three six-step principles. Releasing by topics explores attachment and aversion. Releasing goals works with reactions to a goal. Quick release offers one short invitation.

01 / Start here

Quick release

  1. Notice. Choose one situation. What are you feeling now? An emotion or a sensation such as tightness, heaviness or blankness is enough to start.
  2. Allow. Could you allow the feeling to be here for a moment? Let yourself feel it before you move on.
  3. Release. Ask slowly, “Could I let this feeling go? Would I? When?” Leave space between the questions. Answer honestly, including “no” or “not sure.”
  4. Check. What is here now? Work with the next feeling, pause or choose one useful action.
Start a quick release
02 / Go underneath

The three wants

Here, “wanting” means feeling that you need something before you can be okay. For example, “I need them to like me,” “I need this to go my way,” or “I need to know it will be okay.” Choose the one that sounds closest.

Approval or love

“I need them to like me.” Look for the urge to get praise, agreement or reassurance. Could I let go of wanting approval, just for this moment?

Control

“This has to go my way.” Look for the urge to change someone’s mind or insist on a particular result. Could I let go of wanting control, just for this moment?

Security or survival

“I need a guarantee.” Look for the urge to keep checking or planning until you feel certain. Could I let go of wanting security, just for this moment?

Then ask “Would I? When?” and check what remains. If more than one fits, start with one. You can come back to the others. For example, a brief reply may bring tightness or hurt. Allow that first. If you then notice “I need reassurance,” try the approval prompt.

Explore a want
03 / Find a prompt

Prompts by feeling

Fear or worry
Could I allow this fear? Could I loosen needing certainty right now?
Anger or resentment
Could I allow this anger? Could I loosen the demand that this person or situation be different right now?
Sadness, hurt or rejection
Could I give this sadness some room? If I’m seeking reassurance, could I loosen that need for a moment?
Guilt or self-criticism
Could I let go of attacking myself? Is there something concrete I can do to make things right?
Overthinking or indecision
Could I let go of needing to figure everything out right now? Which decision actually needs my attention?
Procrastination or resistance
Could I allow this “I don’t want to”? Could I loosen the fight with that feeling and choose one manageable action?
Comparison, envy or attachment
Could I loosen needing to prove I’m enough, or needing this exact outcome before I can be okay?

Follow the prompt with “Would I? When?” If it feels off, return to “Could I let this feeling go?”

04 / Make room for difficulty

When you feel stuck

  • If the answer is no, allow the unwillingness. Could you loosen the urge to force yourself?
  • If nothing changes, check for pressure to get a result. Could you let go of wanting to control this stuckness?
  • If you feel blank, notice the blankness itself. If another sensation arrives, start there.
  • If it gets intense, pause. Open your eyes and bring your attention back to the room. You can come back later or stop here.

Lester’s story offers another route through love. Try “Could I soften a little toward myself or this person?” If warmth comes, let yourself feel it. You can keep your boundaries.

Practise with stuckness
05 / The wider practice

The six steps

The wants practice uses the workbook’s self-guided release sequence on printed pages 42–44. After choosing approval, control or safety, it invites you to let go of wanting it so you can experience having it, compare having with wanting, and repeat when the sense of lack remains.

  1. Choose inner steadiness. The workbook calls it “imperturbability.” For this moment, are you willing to loosen your grip on approval, control or security?
  2. Decide to practise. You can give it a try even if you’re unsure.
  3. Notice the underlying want. Does approval, control or security fit? Could you let the want go? Would you? When?
  4. Return throughout the day. Try it during the day when something bothers you.
  5. Release control of stuckness. When you feel blocked, work with the effort to force a release.
  6. Notice what changes. The workbook describes increasing lightness and happiness. Check your own experience and return to the practice when you want to.
06 / Recognise more openness

Courage, acceptance and peace

Courageousness

“I am willing.” You might feel more ready to listen, try another approach or take a small step. Ask “Could I allow a little willingness here?” For a goal statement, try “I allow myself to…” or “I can…”

Acceptance

“I can let this moment be.” You may find yourself arguing less with what happened and thinking more clearly about what to do next. Ask “Could I allow this to be as it is, for this moment?” For a goal statement, try “I have…” if it feels usable.

Peace

“For now, I can rest.” You might notice a little ease while a question is still unresolved. Ask “Could I rest here without trying to get anything?” For a goal statement, try “I am…” if it fits.

Enjoy pleasant feelings when they appear. If “I have to keep this” comes up, use the release questions on that need.

07 / Intention and releasing

Releasing around a goal

I allow myself to [have, enjoy or experience one outcome].

Write one concise, positive statement in the present. Choose a meaningful outcome that feels possible or try a smaller step. Leave room for different routes and for other people’s choices. Keep any action list separate.

  1. Read your statement slowly.
  2. Notice or write the first honest reaction. Try “I don’t deserve it,” “What if I lose it?” or “Having it would put pressure on me” if one fits.
  3. Allow the feeling. Ask “Could I let it go? Would I? When?”
  4. If approval, control or security stands out, use the questions on that want.
  5. Read your statement again. Work with the next reaction, pause or choose one practical action.

Use the statement to notice your relationship to the goal. A practical step might be reviewing a budget, making an enquiry or working on an offer.

08 / Make it your own

Goal examples

Love and partnership

“I allow myself to enjoy a loving, playful, committed partnership.” If you notice “I need someone to choose me,” try releasing the need for approval in this moment. Use this example

Money and savings

“I allow myself to have and enjoy S$_____ in savings.” If you notice “I can’t relax until I have it,” try loosening the need for a guarantee. Use this example

Business and income
